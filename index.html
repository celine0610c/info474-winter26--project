For the 15% of Seattle residents living with a disability, a crumbling curb ramp is not an inconvenience — it is a barrier. When a curb ramp is rated "poor" or "very poor," it can render entire blocks impassable for wheelchair users, people with visual impairments, and parents pushing strollers.

The City of Seattle's Department of Transportation maintains a dataset of every curb ramp in the city, tracking their condition, installation date, and location. What that data reveals is a patchwork of maintenance — some neighborhoods dense with newly installed, compliant ramps, others still relying on infrastructure installed decades ago and long past its useful life.

This investigation examines that data across six dimensions — from the overall breakdown of ramp conditions, to how conditions have shifted over time, to which city districts bear the greatest burden of deteriorating infrastructure.

"A curb ramp rated 'very poor' isn't just a maintenance note — it's a neighborhood telling its residents they don't belong on the street."

When condition data is mapped to neighborhoods, a clear pattern emerges. Districts in south Seattle consistently show higher concentrations of deteriorating ramps than those in wealthier northern neighborhoods — a disparity that mirrors broader patterns of infrastructure disinvestment in lower-income communities of color.

The geography of neglect mirrors the geography of wealth — the neighborhoods least able to advocate for repairs are the ones that need them most.

The data presents Seattle's leadership with a clear challenge: tens of thousands of curb ramps exist across the city, but a significant portion are rated in conditions that compromise their usability for people with disabilities. And the distribution of that deterioration is not random — it follows the fault lines of neighborhood wealth and political power.

What happens next depends on priorities. The data exists. The locations are known. The question is whether the city will act before another generation of residents is left stranded at the curb.

Methodology & Data Notes

All data sourced from the Seattle Open Data Portal's Curb Ramps dataset, maintained by the Seattle Department of Transportation (SDOT). The dataset contains 46,126 records as of the time of analysis.

Condition ratings (Good, Fair, Poor, Very Poor) are assigned by SDOT field inspectors. Records with blank condition fields have been categorized as "Unknown" for visualization purposes. Installation dates were parsed from the INSTALL_DATE field; records with missing or malformed dates were excluded from timeline analysis.

District assignments use the PRIMARYDISTRICTCD field. This project was built with p5.js for the INFO 474 Data Visualization course at the University of Washington.
